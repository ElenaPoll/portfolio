Designing for product

Strategic product design and circular service innovation developed in response to a Barilla brief, centered on active aging, advanced prototyping, and comprehensive multimedia storytelling.

3D MODELING — 3D PRINTING — VIDEO MAKING — MARKET RESEARCH — 2D GRAPHICS — PRODUCT DESIGN

Human-Centered Intelligence & Market Strategy

Commissioned directly through a prestigious design brief by Barilla, this project represents an exhaustive exploration into inclusive product development and sustainable packaging systems tailored for the elderly demographic. The primary challenge demanded a deep synthesis of industrial manufacturing constraints and ergonomic sensitivity, shifting the design focus toward accessibility, cognitive clarity, and independence for third-age users.

To establish an uncompromised foundation of empirical evidence, I led an intensive, multi-layered market research campaign. This involved conducting immersive shadowing sessions and contextual qualitative interviews with senior citizens at a local community center in Mornese. By closely observing everyday rituals, motor limitations, and consumption habits, alongside mapping competitive benchmarks in large-scale retail packaging, I was able to isolate critical unmet needs and translate them into actionable design drivers.

From Concept Architecture to 3D Prototyping

The resulting design solution is an innovative single-serving breakfast cup engineered specifically for liquid bases (milk or yogurt) combined with dry cereals. The concept introduces a groundbreaking circular service model: once the content is consumed, the outer container is structurally reconfigured to serve as a reusable planting box, equipped with organic seeds integrated into its rear compartment. This closes the product lifecycle loop, transforming food packaging into an engaging, sustainable gardening activity.

My methodological execution spanned across multiple advanced competencies: starting from meticulous 2D graphics and branding guidelines for clear visual communication, moving into sophisticated 3D modeling environments to optimize wall thicknesses, snap-fit mechanisms, and structural integrity. The iterative phase culminated in functional physical testing through 3D printing prototyping, evaluated directly through usability sessions with students from UniGe's third-age programs to fine-tune opening forces and haptic feedback.
